# Hook Bank

## Purpose of this file
Use this as a bank of reusable hook patterns, not finished hooks. Hooks should reveal a thinking flaw, not lead with a tool.

## Hook rules
- Start with the operator problem or hidden flaw.
- Make the mental model feel necessary.
- Avoid tool-led hooks.
- Avoid hype, fake urgency, and income claims.
- Keep the tone sharp, calm, and useful.

## Pattern 1: Hidden flaw
- **Formula:** You think the problem is [surface issue]. The real problem is [hidden thinking flaw].
- **When to use:** When the audience is solving the wrong layer of the problem.
- **Bad version:** Use these AI tools to fix your workflow.
- **Better version:** You think the workflow is slow. The real problem is that no one defined the decision layer.

## Pattern 2: Contrarian operator truth
- **Formula:** Good operators do not [common behavior]. They [better operating behavior].
- **When to use:** When the post teaches senior operator judgment.
- **Bad version:** Top operators use these productivity apps.
- **Better version:** Good operators do not create more updates. They create decision-ready clarity.

## Pattern 3: Broken system
- **Formula:** If [system] depends on [fragile behavior], you do not have a system. You have [failure mode].
- **When to use:** When the audience is relying on memory, effort, or scattered follow-ups.
- **Bad version:** Automate your business with this tool.
- **Better version:** If your follow-up system depends on memory, you do not have a system. You have a recurring leak.

## Pattern 4: Decision layer
- **Formula:** [Artifact] is not useful because it has more information. It is useful when it helps [decision].
- **When to use:** For MIS, reports, dashboards, decks, updates, and reviews.
- **Bad version:** Build better dashboards with AI.
- **Better version:** A report is not useful because it has more data. It is useful when it helps someone decide.

## Pattern 5: Evidence gap
- **Formula:** You may be doing the work. But if you are not capturing [evidence type], [stakeholder] cannot see [value].
- **When to use:** For appraisal, fundraising, stakeholder updates, and visibility topics.
- **Bad version:** Use this template to get promoted.
- **Better version:** You may be doing the work. But if you are not capturing evidence, your impact stays invisible.

## Pattern 6: Senior operator thinking
- **Formula:** Junior operators [lower-level behavior]. Senior operators [higher-level behavior].
- **When to use:** When teaching how to operate above role or title.
- **Bad version:** Become more productive with these hacks.
- **Better version:** Junior operators report activity. Senior operators explain tradeoffs, risks, and next decisions.

## Pattern 7: Leverage over grind
- **Formula:** More [effort] will not fix [system problem]. You need [leverage mechanism].
- **When to use:** When the audience is overworking because the system is unclear.
- **Bad version:** Work harder and stay consistent.
- **Better version:** More meetings will not fix unclear ownership. You need an operating map.

## Plain-language-first hook rules
Hooks should create tension in words the reader would actually use.

Rules:
- Say the simple problem before introducing any framework name.
- Avoid hooks that sound like internal strategy documentation.
- Avoid finance/fundraising jargon unless the hook translates it immediately.
- Do not lead with a decorative framework title when a plain insight is clearer.
- A smart reader should understand the hook without decoding our terminology.

Hook tests:
- Could a smart reader explain this slide without using our terminology?
- Does this slide read like a useful insight, not a strategy document?
- Did we explain the problem before naming the framework?
- Would simpler wording make this more powerful?

## Current hook guardrails

Hooks must support Plain Language First and One Carousel, One Argument Rule. A good hook names the business/work tension in plain English, implies the practical consequence, and opens the Connected Narrative Spine. Avoid hooks that sound clever but do not tell the reader what situation the carousel helps them understand.
