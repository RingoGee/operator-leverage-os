# Design Prompts: Capital Concentration Proof Stack

## Overall visual direction
Create an 8-slide infographic-led carousel that feels like an operating diagram for fundraising communication. The central visual object is a capital allocation funnel that becomes a five-layer proof stack.

## Premium analytical identity
- Boardroom-ready, calm, sharp, high-signal.
- Looks like a visual strategy memo, not a motivational fundraising post.
- Visuals explain investor allocation logic; text supports the diagram.

## Locked slide chrome system
- Top-left: small section label, e.g., “Capital & Narrative”.
- Top-right: slide number in `07 / 08` style.
- Bottom-left: `Operator Leverage OS`.
- Bottom-right: small source/caveat marker where evidence appears.
- Keep chrome quiet and consistent.

## Visual identity direction
- Warm off-white background.
- Charcoal primary type and lines.
- Deep navy or forest accent for “proof.”
- One electric accent for the attention bottleneck.
- Muted red only for doubts or leakage.

## Signature accent palette
- Background: warm ivory / light sand.
- Primary: charcoal.
- Accent 1: deep navy or forest green.
- Accent 2: electric blue for bottleneck highlights.
- Warning: muted red sparingly.

## Typography direction
- Strong condensed headline for slide tension.
- Clean sans-serif labels for diagrams.
- Small monospace-style tags for source labels and framework layers.
- No paragraph-heavy slides.

## Mobile readability rules
- One main visual object per slide.
- Use labels instead of long copy.
- Maintain strong whitespace around the central diagram.
- Every slide should be readable at three levels: headline, diagram labels, source/caveat details.

## Slide-by-slide visual directions

### Slide 1
- **Composition archetype:** Funnel tension visual.
- **Dominant element:** Large capital pool narrowing into a tiny attention gate.
- **Headline mode:** Contrarian market tension.
- **Visual direction:** Draw a wide top funnel labeled “capital available” narrowing sharply into “investor attention.” Place headline above or beside it.
- **Minimal slide text:** “Capital is available. Attention is not.” / “Are you allocatable?”

### Slide 2
- **Composition archetype:** Bottleneck map.
- **Dominant element:** Three gates: plausible, diligence-worthy, allocation-ready.
- **Headline mode:** Diagnostic statement.
- **Visual direction:** Use a left-to-right filter system showing many small company dots narrowing through gates.
- **Minimal slide text:** “Good is not enough. The question is allocation readiness.”

### Slide 3
- **Composition archetype:** Evidence stack.
- **Dominant element:** Three evidence cards.
- **Headline mode:** Evidence label.
- **Visual direction:** Place source-backed cards in a clean grid under a header: “Market signal: more dollars, fewer rounds.” Add a caveat strip at bottom.
- **Minimal slide text:** Carta funding, Carta round count, NVCA AI concentration. Keep exact numbers legible.

### Slide 4
- **Composition archetype:** Split-screen contrast.
- **Dominant element:** Two deck cards.
- **Headline mode:** Antithesis.
- **Visual direction:** Left card is busier and labeled “louder deck.” Right card is structured and labeled “proof stack.”
- **Minimal slide text:** “More impressive ≠ more investable.”

### Slide 5
- **Composition archetype:** Mechanism conveyor.
- **Dominant element:** Doubt-removal conveyor.
- **Headline mode:** Mechanism reveal.
- **Visual direction:** Show investor doubts entering a conveyor and coming out as checked proof blocks.
- **Minimal slide text:** “Strong slide = one doubt removed.”

### Slide 6
- **Composition archetype:** Layered framework.
- **Dominant element:** Five-layer Allocation Proof Stack.
- **Headline mode:** Named model.
- **Visual direction:** Stack five thick layers vertically, each with one diagnostic question.
- **Minimal slide text:** “Market pull / Problem urgency / Traction quality / Defensibility / Capital logic.”

### Slide 7
- **Composition archetype:** Before/after teardown.
- **Dominant element:** Rewritten narrative card.
- **Headline mode:** Applied rewrite.
- **Visual direction:** Left: generic category sentence. Right: allocation case with annotation tags from the proof stack.
- **Minimal slide text:** “From category claim → allocation case.”

### Slide 8
- **Composition archetype:** Operator checklist.
- **Dominant element:** One operating question card.
- **Headline mode:** Behavior shift.
- **Visual direction:** Large checklist card with the proof stack ghosted behind it.
- **Minimal slide text:** “Which investor doubt does this remove?”

## Evidence slide direction
Use source tags for Carta and NVCA. Include caveat: “Market signal, not a company-level guarantee.”

## Antithesis slide direction
Keep the contrast respectful. The weaker side should look noisy, not foolish.

## Framework slide direction
Slide 6 should be the most save-worthy slide. Make the proof stack clean enough to screenshot.

## Application slide direction
Make the before/after copy legible. Use annotation labels, not long explanation.

## Final profile-entry slide direction
No aggressive CTA. Use a calm footer: “Operator Leverage OS — visual systems for strategy, investor, and operator clarity.”

## What to avoid
- Generic startup stock images.
- Rocket ships, money bags, or hype visuals.
- AI icons as the main hook.
- Fake dashboards.
- Dense paragraphs.
- Claims that fundraising is easy because AI is hot.
