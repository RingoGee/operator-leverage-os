# Visual Object Bank

## Purpose of this file
Use this file to choose the central visual object for a carousel. The visual object must explain the thinking, not decorate the slide.

## Selection rule
Pick one primary visual object per carousel. If the idea needs three different visual systems to work, the idea is probably too broad.

## Visual objects

### Decision tree
- **Explains best:** How to choose between paths under uncertainty.
- **Best-fit pillars:** Strategy Systems, Corporate Leverage, Capital & Narrative.
- **Use when:** The audience needs a sequence of if/then decisions.
- **Do not use when:** The problem is about evidence, rhythm, or narrative rather than choice.
- **Example operator problem:** A team has three possible GTM moves and no clear decision logic.

### Control panel
- **Explains best:** The few signals an operator should monitor to run a system.
- **Best-fit pillars:** Founder Leverage, Corporate Leverage, Strategy Systems.
- **Use when:** The problem is recurring and needs ongoing operating visibility.
- **Do not use when:** The idea is a one-time decision or narrative teardown.
- **Example operator problem:** A founder is tracking too many metrics and still lacks clarity.

### Evidence bank
- **Explains best:** How to collect proof of work, progress, or impact.
- **Best-fit pillars:** Corporate Leverage, Capital & Narrative.
- **Use when:** The audience needs to make invisible work visible.
- **Do not use when:** The problem is primarily about choosing between options.
- **Example operator problem:** A manager has strong output but weak appraisal evidence.

### Proof stack
- **Explains best:** How different layers of proof build trust.
- **Best-fit pillars:** Capital & Narrative, Founder Leverage.
- **Use when:** The audience needs to turn traction, work, or claims into credible evidence.
- **Do not use when:** The problem is a simple workflow or checklist.
- **Example operator problem:** A founder has traction points but no investor-ready proof structure.

### Narrative spine
- **Explains best:** The logic that connects context, change, evidence, and ask.
- **Best-fit pillars:** Capital & Narrative, Corporate Leverage.
- **Use when:** The issue is communication clarity, not execution mechanics.
- **Do not use when:** The idea is mainly about operations or metrics monitoring.
- **Example operator problem:** An investor update lists activity but does not create confidence.

### Operating map
- **Explains best:** How work flows across people, inputs, decisions, and outputs.
- **Best-fit pillars:** Strategy Systems, Founder Leverage, Corporate Leverage.
- **Use when:** The audience needs to see how a system works end to end.
- **Do not use when:** The idea can be explained with a simpler scorecard or ladder.
- **Example operator problem:** A cross-functional project keeps stalling because ownership is unclear.

### Scorecard
- **Explains best:** How to evaluate quality, readiness, maturity, or fit.
- **Best-fit pillars:** All pillars.
- **Use when:** The audience needs to diagnose whether something is strong or weak.
- **Do not use when:** The problem is about sequence or causality.
- **Example operator problem:** A proposal looks polished but does not show enough strategic clarity.

### Ladder
- **Explains best:** Progression from low maturity to high maturity.
- **Best-fit pillars:** Founder Leverage, Corporate Leverage, Strategy Systems.
- **Use when:** The audience needs to understand levels, upgrades, or maturity stages.
- **Do not use when:** The idea is not sequential or hierarchical.
- **Example operator problem:** A professional wants to move from task execution to strategic ownership.

### Funnel
- **Explains best:** Conversion, filtering, prioritization, or narrowing choices.
- **Best-fit pillars:** Strategy Systems, Founder Leverage.
- **Use when:** The audience needs to reduce many inputs into fewer useful outputs.
- **Do not use when:** The problem is about trust, evidence, or stakeholder alignment.
- **Example operator problem:** A founder has too many leads but no clear qualification logic.

### Loop
- **Explains best:** Recurring feedback, operating rhythm, or compounding process.
- **Best-fit pillars:** Founder Leverage, Strategy Systems.
- **Use when:** The idea is cyclic and improves through review.
- **Do not use when:** The problem is a one-way narrative or decision sequence.
- **Example operator problem:** A weekly review creates notes but no operational learning.

### Before/after system
- **Explains best:** How a messy way of working changes after applying the model.
- **Best-fit pillars:** All pillars.
- **Use when:** Contrast will make the mental model easier to understand.
- **Do not use when:** The after-state is not meaningfully different from the before-state.
- **Example operator problem:** A status update becomes a decision-ready stakeholder note.

### Teardown map
- **Explains best:** Why a document, update, workflow, or decision process fails.
- **Best-fit pillars:** Strategy Systems, Capital & Narrative, Corporate Leverage.
- **Use when:** The audience can learn by seeing failure points marked clearly.
- **Do not use when:** The idea is new teaching without a concrete object to examine.
- **Example operator problem:** A business review deck has data but no decision logic.

### Decision layer
- **Explains best:** The difference between data, interpretation, options, tradeoffs, and action.
- **Best-fit pillars:** Strategy Systems, Corporate Leverage.
- **Use when:** The audience is confusing reporting with decision-making.
- **Do not use when:** The issue is mostly emotional motivation or personal productivity.
- **Example operator problem:** An MIS report shows numbers but does not help leaders decide.

### Visibility panel
- **Explains best:** How to make contribution, progress, and impact legible to stakeholders.
- **Best-fit pillars:** Corporate Leverage, Founder Leverage.
- **Use when:** The audience needs structured proof without bragging.
- **Do not use when:** The problem is about investor narrative or GTM logic.
- **Example operator problem:** A high-performing employee is invisible in appraisal conversations.

### Work-to-impact chain
- **Explains best:** How activities connect to outcomes, proof, and strategic value.
- **Best-fit pillars:** Corporate Leverage, Strategy Systems, Capital & Narrative.
- **Use when:** The audience needs to show why work mattered.
- **Do not use when:** The idea is only about organizing tasks.
- **Example operator problem:** A team lists completed work but cannot explain business impact.
