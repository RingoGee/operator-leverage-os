# Design Prompts: Two Markets, One Headline

## Purpose
Create a design-ready visual prompt for a Capital & Narrative carousel about reading venture market headlines correctly. This file is for visual direction only. Do not generate images, designed slides, Canva files, or visual execution packs from this step.

## Working title
The Headline Says “VC Is Back.” Your Market May Not Be.

## One-sentence point
Founders should separate headline venture-market recovery from their specific funding lane, because AI mega-deals and concentrated capital can make the market look broad when attention is still narrow.

## Current source context
Use this context carefully and with caveats:

- NVCA's 2026 Yearbook says 2025 U.S. VC deal value reached `$320B`, while AI represented `65.4%` of deal value.
- NVCA's 2026 Yearbook describes a market where mega-deals and nontraditional investors made headline totals look stronger than the broader funding environment.
- NVCA's Q1 2026 PitchBook-NVCA Venture Monitor summary says Q1 deal value and exit value were heavily concentrated in the five largest deals/exits.
- KPMG's Q1 2026 Venture Pulse also describes record global VC investment driven by AI megadeals and concentrated capital.

Sources:

- NVCA, 2026 Yearbook: https://nvca.org/2026-nvca-yearbook/
- NVCA, Q1 2026 PitchBook-NVCA Venture Monitor: https://nvca.org/pitchbook-nvca-venture-monitor/
- KPMG, Venture Pulse Q1 2026: https://kpmg.com/us/en/articles/2026/venture-pulse-q1-2026.html

## Brand Identity Lock v1
Use the locked Operator Leverage OS identity:

- **Palette:** Warm Ivory / Soft Paper White background; Deep Charcoal / Near Black text and linework; Dark Forest Green main accent; Slate Blue evidence cards; Proof Orange / Controlled Amber for gates and key questions; Muted Red / Rust for false read or wrong market assumption; Deep Green for useful interpretation.
- **Typography:** display serif for major conceptual headlines; clean modern sans-serif for body, labels, diagrams, notes, and source lines; optional mono-style only for source tags, memo codes, or field-manual markers.
- **Chrome:** stable top-left `Capital & Narrative`, top-right slide number, optional memo/category label, bottom-left `Operator Leverage OS`, optional footer source/caveat line on evidence slide only, consistent safe margins and baseline alignment.
- **Spacing:** generous margins, clear separation between headline, explanation, and visual object, mobile-first sizing, no unreadable tiny labels.
- **Diagram language:** clean line diagrams, restrained icons, consistent line weights, structured cards, market maps, filters, and split-screen comparisons.
- **Surface treatment:** warm paper-like background, subtle texture only, soft minimal shadows, meaningful stamps/labels/manual marks only.
- **Allowed variation:** vary slide archetype, evidence treatment, map shape, headline tone, or single-post accent emphasis. Do not invent a new visual identity.

## Premium analytical identity
The carousel should feel like a market-reading memo for founders: not pessimistic, not hype-driven, and not academic. It should help a founder ask, “Which market am I actually in?”

## Locked slide chrome system
- Top-left: `Capital & Narrative`.
- Top-right: `01 / 08` through `08 / 08`.
- Bottom-left: `Operator Leverage OS`.
- Bottom-right: source/caveat marker only on Slide 3.
- Keep chrome quiet and fixed.

## Visual identity direction
Use a two-layer market map. One layer shows the loud headline market; the other shows the founder's actual lane. The central visual object is a headline split test: headline signal vs founder-specific relevance.

## Signature accent palette
- Dark Forest Green: useful read / founder-specific market truth.
- Slate Blue: evidence and source context.
- Controlled Amber: filtering questions.
- Muted Red / Rust: misleading headline read.
- Deep Green: sharper fundraising narrative.

## Typography direction
- Display serif: Slide 1 headline and Slide 6 framework title only.
- Clean sans-serif: labels, questions, source notes, and support text.
- Mono-style: source tags and small caveat labels only.

## Diagram family
Two-market map + headline filter + application rewrite card.

## Plain-language shadows for framework terms
- Headline market → what the news says is happening.
- Your funding lane → the investors, stage, sector, and proof standard that apply to you.
- Capital concentration → money clustering around a small set of companies or categories.
- False read → assuming the headline applies to your company.
- Useful read → translating the headline into what your investor needs to believe.

## Mobile readability rules
- Keep the two-market map visually obvious.
- Use short labels: `headline market` and `your lane`.
- Evidence cards should carry one number or one implication each.
- Avoid tiny sector/category taxonomies.
- Make Slide 6 usable as a decision filter.

## Slide-by-slide visual directions

### Slide 1 — Tension headline
- **Role:** hook.
- **Reader value delivered:** the reader sees why “VC is back” can be a misleading operating assumption.
- **Composition archetype:** headline card split by a market filter.
- **Dominant element:** giant press-style headline card cut into two paths: `headline market` and `your funding lane`.
- **Headline mode:** display-serif tension.
- **Signature visual grammar:** rust warning line on the false-read side, green filter on the useful-read side.
- **Minimal slide text:** “The headline says ‘VC is back.’ Your market may not be.”

### Slide 2 — The two-market map
- **Role:** diagnosis.
- **Reader value delivered:** a simple map for separating broad market signal from founder-specific reality.
- **Composition archetype:** stacked market map.
- **Dominant element:** top layer `Headline Market` with big deal bubbles; bottom layer `Your Lane` with stage, sector, traction, investor fit.
- **Headline mode:** plain-language diagnosis.
- **Signature visual grammar:** transparent overlay, labels, and a controlled amber “Do not assume transfer” gate.
- **Minimal slide text:** “There is the market in the headline. Then there is the market you raise in.”

### Slide 3 — Evidence cards
- **Role:** evidence slide direction.
- **Reader value delivered:** current context without turning the carousel into a market report.
- **Composition archetype:** evidence bank.
- **Dominant element:** three Slate Blue cards.
- **Cards:** `NVCA: $320B deployed in 2025`; `NVCA: AI was 65.4% of deal value`; `Q1 2026: largest deals/exits drove headline values`.
- **Headline mode:** factual context.
- **Signature visual grammar:** source tags, caveat footer, no dense charts.
- **Caveat:** “Market-level signal. Translate before applying.”

### Slide 4 — False read vs useful read
- **Role:** antithesis slide direction.
- **Reader value delivered:** the reader learns the wrong and right way to use market headlines.
- **Composition archetype:** split-screen comparison.
- **Dominant element:** left rust `False read` card vs right green `Useful read` card.
- **Headline mode:** contrast headline.
- **Left text:** “Capital is flowing, so fundraising should be easier.”
- **Right text:** “Capital is flowing to specific proof profiles. Which one do we match?”
- **Signature visual grammar:** rust assumption stamp; green translation stamp.

### Slide 5 — Founder consequence
- **Role:** hidden mechanism.
- **Reader value delivered:** the reader sees why headline optimism can weaken fundraising materials.
- **Composition archetype:** cause-and-effect chain.
- **Dominant element:** headline optimism → broad deck → unclear investor fit → weak conviction.
- **Headline mode:** mechanism reveal.
- **Signature visual grammar:** rust leakage arrows turning into a green filter path.
- **Minimal slide text:** “A broad market story can make your specific case blurrier.”

### Slide 6 — The Headline Split Test
- **Role:** framework slide direction.
- **Reader value delivered:** a reusable filter for market headlines.
- **Composition archetype:** four-question diagnostic panel.
- **Dominant element:** field-manual card with four gates.
- **Questions:** `Which category is getting capital?` `Which stage?` `Which proof standard?` `Which investor behavior changes for us?`
- **Headline mode:** display-serif framework title.
- **Signature visual grammar:** amber gates, green passed checks, mono-style question numbers.
- **Minimal slide text:** “Do not ask if VC is back. Ask which VC market is back.”

### Slide 7 — Application rewrite
- **Role:** application slide direction.
- **Reader value delivered:** a practical way to rewrite a fundraising narrative.
- **Composition archetype:** before/after memo card.
- **Dominant element:** before sentence becomes filtered after sentence.
- **Before:** “The market is improving.”
- **After:** “Capital is concentrating around companies with clear category pull and defensible proof. Our narrative should show why we fit that proof standard.”
- **Headline mode:** practical instruction.
- **Signature visual grammar:** annotate `category`, `proof`, and `fit` in the after sentence.
- **Note:** “Fictional example for structure.”

### Slide 8 — Final operating rule
- **Role:** final profile-entry slide direction.
- **Reader value delivered:** a simple question to apply before using market data in a deck or update.
- **Composition archetype:** one-question field manual card.
- **Dominant element:** amber question card with small green filter icon.
- **Headline mode:** direct operating question.
- **Main text:** “Which part of the headline actually applies to us?”
- **Profile line:** “Operator Leverage OS — visual systems for strategy, investor, and operator clarity.”

## Evidence slide direction
Slide 3 must use evidence to show concentration, not to make a prediction. Keep the caveat visible enough to prevent overclaiming.

## Antithesis slide direction
Slide 4 should compare a sloppy headline interpretation with a sharper operating interpretation. The tone should be corrective, not cynical.

## Framework slide direction
Slide 6 should be the most reusable slide. It should function as a filter founders can apply whenever a venture-market headline appears.

## Application slide direction
Slide 7 should show how to turn macro context into a more precise fundraising narrative. Keep the example fictional and clearly labeled.

## Final profile-entry slide direction
Slide 8 should position Operator Leverage OS as a place for clearer investor and operator thinking, not as a paid-product pitch.

## What to avoid
- “VC is back / VC is dead” binary thinking.
- Generic market commentary without founder action.
- Overloaded category maps.
- Random AI-tool imagery.
- Doom, hype, or prediction language.
- New palette, decorative gradients, or stock market imagery.
- Claims that funding will be easy or impossible.
