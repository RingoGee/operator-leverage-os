# Carousel Brief: The Decision Layer

## Working title
The Decision Layer

## Pillar
Strategy Systems

## Audience segment
Strategy and growth operators, founder's office and chief-of-staff professionals, corporate professionals, consultants, and advisory teams who need to make work decision-ready.

## Real operator problem
Operators share information, analysis, and updates, but senior stakeholders still ask, “So what should we do?”

## Hidden thinking flaw
They think analysis is the final output. For senior stakeholders, analysis is only useful when it becomes a decision layer: context, options, tradeoffs, recommendation, and next action.

## Mental model
Decision layer: Information → Analysis → Options → Tradeoffs → Recommendation → Action.

## Central visual object
Decision layer.

## Why this topic belongs early in the launch sequence
This is one of the clearest Strategy Systems ideas and directly supports the brand promise: turning messy work into clear systems. It also reinforces that the brand is about operator judgment, not tool usage.

## Recommended 7-slide visual sequence

| Slide | Visual purpose | Visual object or layout | Minimal slide text direction | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Show the stakeholder gap | Update/report ending in an unanswered decision | Short problem label | Show the “so what?” moment visually. |
| 2 | Reveal the hidden flaw | Analysis layer stopping before decision layer | One sentence naming the flaw | Keep it about structure, not intelligence. |
| 3 | Introduce the mental model | Decision layer stack | Name the model | Show stacked layers. |
| 4 | Show the full decision layer | Information → Analysis → Options → Tradeoffs → Recommendation → Action | Labels only | This is the central visual object. |
| 5 | Apply to one recommendation | Raw analysis transformed into decision-ready note | Short before/after labels | Use a business review or project recommendation. |
| 6 | Show the behavior shift | From explaining data to designing decisions | One practical takeaway | Emphasize senior operator communication. |
| 7 | Save/apply/bridge | Decision layer as reusable filter | Save/apply pattern | Optional bridge to decision memo, not Proof Stack unless proof capture is relevant. |

## Minimal slide text direction
Keep each layer to one or two words. Use arrows or stacked blocks to show movement from information to action.

## Small applied example
A retention analysis becomes decision-ready when it includes the metric, what changed, likely causes, available options, tradeoffs, a recommendation, and the action owner.

## Optional paid asset bridge to Proof Stack
- **Paid asset bridge:** Not primary for this brief.
- **If used:** Proof Stack may support the evidence side of decision-ready updates, but this brief should mainly teach the decision layer itself.
- **Boundary:** Do not force a product bridge if the free mental model is stronger without it.

## CTA pattern, not final CTA copy

## Visual notes
- Use a stack, layer cake, or vertical progression.
- Avoid spreadsheet or analytics-tool visuals.
- Make the distinction between information, analysis, and decision-readiness visually obvious.
