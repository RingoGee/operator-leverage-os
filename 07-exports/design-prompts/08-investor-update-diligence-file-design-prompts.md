# Design Prompts: The Investor Update Is Becoming a Diligence File

## Overall visual direction
Create an 8-slide infographic-led carousel built around one central visual object: an investor update email transforming into a structured diligence file and then into a six-part update control panel.

## Premium analytical identity
- Calm, precise, governance-grade.
- Should feel like a founder's office operating system for investor communication.
- Visuals should communicate continuity, comparability, and trust.

## Locked slide chrome system
- Top-left: “Capital & Narrative”.
- Top-right: slide number.
- Bottom-left: `Operator Leverage OS`.
- Bottom-right: source/caveat note where relevant.
- Use the same header/footer placement across all slides.

## Visual identity direction
- Warm document background.
- Charcoal linework.
- Deep blue or green accent for trusted continuity.
- Muted red for broken timelines or KPI drift.
- Source cards should feel like evidence, not decoration.

## Signature accent palette
- Background: warm ivory.
- Primary: charcoal.
- Trust accent: deep blue / deep green.
- Warning accent: muted red.
- Neutral cards: light gray or sand.

## Typography direction
- Crisp headlines.
- Modular labels.
- Monospace-style data tags for metrics and source labels.
- Avoid long update paragraphs except on the before/after slide.

## Mobile readability rules
- Each slide must have one primary reading zone.
- Use timeline and folder metaphors large enough for mobile.
- Keep the six-part framework visually clean and screenshot-ready.

## Slide-by-slide visual directions

### Slide 1
- **Composition archetype:** Transformation visual.
- **Dominant element:** Email card becoming a diligence folder.
- **Headline mode:** Reframe.
- **Visual direction:** Show a monthly email card folding into a structured folder labeled “future diligence file.”
- **Minimal slide text:** “Your investor update is not an email.” / “It is diligence before diligence.”

### Slide 2
- **Composition archetype:** Broken timeline.
- **Dominant element:** KPI drift line.
- **Headline mode:** Diagnosis.
- **Visual direction:** Show three monthly cards with mismatched metrics, missing context, and changed definitions.
- **Minimal slide text:** “Random updates create trust gaps.”

### Slide 3
- **Composition archetype:** Evidence stack.
- **Dominant element:** Source-backed cards connected to a data-room icon.
- **Headline mode:** Evidence label.
- **Visual direction:** Two evidence cards: AI-assisted diligence from PitchBook/Web Summit; structured reporting expectations from Findex. Add caveat: “AI-assisted does not mean fully automated.”
- **Minimal slide text:** “Structured inputs matter more, not less.”

### Slide 4
- **Composition archetype:** Antithesis table.
- **Dominant element:** Pretty update vs trusted record.
- **Headline mode:** Antithesis.
- **Visual direction:** Left card: polished but unstable. Right card: boring but comparable and trusted.
- **Minimal slide text:** “Pretty update ≠ trusted record.”

### Slide 5
- **Composition archetype:** Mechanism spine.
- **Dominant element:** Connected monthly update cards.
- **Headline mode:** Mechanism reveal.
- **Visual direction:** A single continuity spine runs through six monthly updates with stable fields.
- **Minimal slide text:** “Trust compounds through continuity.”

### Slide 6
- **Composition archetype:** Control panel framework.
- **Dominant element:** Six-part Diligence Trail Update.
- **Headline mode:** Named framework.
- **Visual direction:** Six equal modules arranged in a clean 2x3 grid.
- **Minimal slide text:** Baseline / Movement / Meaning / Risk / Ask / Next proof.

### Slide 7
- **Composition archetype:** Before/after teardown.
- **Dominant element:** Update rewrite card.
- **Headline mode:** Application.
- **Visual direction:** Show the weak activity update on the left and the decision-context update on the right, with tags for movement, meaning, risk, next proof.
- **Minimal slide text:** “Activity reporting → decision context.”

### Slide 8
- **Composition archetype:** Timeline checklist.
- **Dominant element:** Six-update reader timeline.
- **Headline mode:** Behavior shift.
- **Visual direction:** Show six update cards leading to a trust signal.
- **Minimal slide text:** “Write for the six-update reader.”

## Evidence slide direction
Use clear labels:
- “PitchBook/Web Summit: AI is entering diligence workflows.”
- “Findex: precision, structure, and consistency beat volume.”
Add caveat that the carousel is not claiming automation replaces judgment.

## Antithesis slide direction
Make the weaker side visibly inconsistent; make the stronger side visibly comparable.

## Framework slide direction
Slide 6 must work as a standalone saved template.

## Application slide direction
Use a fictional example label. Keep it clear that the example demonstrates structure, not a real company claim.

## Final profile-entry slide direction
End with a soft profile reason: “Systems for sharper investor and operator communication.”

## What to avoid
- Tool-led visuals.
- SaaS dashboard screenshots.
- Overclaiming that investor updates guarantee funding.
- Making the update look like a legal document.
- Text-heavy email mockups.

## Design QA checklist
- [ ] Continuity is visible across slides.
- [ ] The six-part framework is screenshot-ready.
- [ ] Evidence is source-labeled and caveated.
- [ ] The visual object is explanatory, not decorative.
- [ ] No images or designed slides are generated in this file.
