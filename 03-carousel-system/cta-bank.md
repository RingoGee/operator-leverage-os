# CTA Bank

## Purpose of this file
Use this as a bank of CTA patterns that invite saving, thinking, discussion, diagnosis, application, or paid implementation without hype.

## CTA rules
- Keep CTAs calm and useful.
- Do not use fake urgency.
- Do not use comment-bait.
- Do not imply secret prompts or hidden tricks.
- If there is a paid bridge, make the boundary clear: free model first, paid implementation second.

## Category 1: Save for later
- **Purpose:** Encourage the reader to reuse the visual model.
- **Pattern:** Save this for the next time you need to [operator situation].
- **Use when:** The carousel contains a reusable framework, scorecard, map, or decision tree.
- **Avoid:** Save this before it disappears.

## Category 2: Diagnose your work
- **Purpose:** Invite the reader to inspect their own workflow, update, report, or decision process.
- **Pattern:** Use this to check whether your [work artifact] is missing [thinking layer].
- **Use when:** The carousel teaches a diagnostic or scorecard.
- **Avoid:** Comment if you are guilty of this.

## Category 3: Discuss the model
- **Purpose:** Create thoughtful conversation around the mental model.
- **Pattern:** Which layer breaks most often in your work: [layer A], [layer B], or [layer C]?
- **Use when:** The carousel has clear parts or layers worth discussing.
- **Avoid:** Comment YES if you agree.

## Category 4: Apply the framework
- **Purpose:** Push the reader from understanding to one small action.
- **Pattern:** Apply this to one [meeting, update, proposal, report, decision] this week.
- **Use when:** The post includes a small applied example.
- **Avoid:** Transform your entire business today.

## Category 5: Paid asset bridge
- **Purpose:** Point to implementation support without making the free post feel incomplete.
- **Pattern:** The free post teaches the model. The paid asset helps you implement it faster with [template/system/canvas/tracker/SOP].
- **Use when:** There is a clear implementation asset connected to the model.
- **Avoid:** DM me for the secret prompt.

## CTA approval checklist
- [ ] The CTA matches the reader's stage.
- [ ] The CTA does not use hype or fake urgency.
- [ ] The CTA does not make the tool the hero.
- [ ] The CTA invites saving, thinking, discussion, diagnosis, application, or implementation.
- [ ] Any paid CTA keeps free teaching separate from paid execution.

## Plain-language CTA rules
CTAs should continue the narrative instead of sounding like a separate sales block.

Rules:
- Use direct, helpful language.
- Name the next behavior the reader should try.
- Keep paid-asset bridges implementation-led, not hype-led.
- Do not introduce new jargon on the final slide.
- The CTA should feel like the natural next step after the carousel’s argument.

CTA tests:
- Does the next step feel inevitable?
- Does the CTA read like a useful next action, not a strategy document?
- Would simpler wording make this more powerful?
