# Design Prompts: Liquidity Backlog Investor Narrative

## Purpose
Create a design-ready visual prompt for a Capital & Narrative carousel about investor liquidity pressure and founder communication. This file is for visual direction only. Do not generate images, designed slides, Canva files, or visual execution packs from this step.

## Working title
Your Investor Has a Liquidity Problem Too

## One-sentence point
Founders should connect progress to credible value-realization steps because investors are making new funding decisions while exits, distributions, and liquidity remain uneven.

## Current source context
Use this context carefully and with caveats:

- NVCA's Q1 2026 PitchBook-NVCA Venture Monitor summary says Q1 2026 set record deal and exit values, but those totals were highly concentrated in the five largest deals/exits.
- The same NVCA summary says liquidity is still tight, AI continues to dominate, and volatility adds obstacles to an already difficult exit environment.
- NVCA's 2026 Yearbook discusses liquidity pressure across exits, LP distributions, fundraising, and the unicorn backlog.
- KPMG's Q1 2026 Venture Pulse describes uneven exit conditions and capital concentration in AI megadeals.

Sources:

- NVCA, Q1 2026 PitchBook-NVCA Venture Monitor: https://nvca.org/pitchbook-nvca-venture-monitor/
- NVCA, 2026 Yearbook: https://nvca.org/2026-nvca-yearbook/
- KPMG, Venture Pulse Q1 2026: https://kpmg.com/us/en/articles/2026/venture-pulse-q1-2026.html

## Brand Identity Lock v1
Use the locked Operator Leverage OS identity:

- **Palette:** Warm Ivory / Soft Paper White background; Deep Charcoal / Near Black text and linework; Dark Forest Green main accent; Slate Blue evidence cards; Proof Orange / Controlled Amber for gates and key questions; Muted Red / Rust for liquidity friction; Deep Green for credible value path.
- **Typography:** display serif for major conceptual headlines; clean modern sans-serif for body, labels, diagrams, notes, and source lines; optional mono-style only for source tags, memo codes, or field-manual markers.
- **Chrome:** stable top-left `Capital & Narrative`, top-right slide number, optional memo/category label, bottom-left `Operator Leverage OS`, optional footer source/caveat line on evidence slide only, consistent safe margins and baseline alignment.
- **Spacing:** generous margins, clear separation between headline, explanation, and visual object, mobile-first sizing, no unreadable tiny labels.
- **Diagram language:** clean line diagrams, restrained icons, consistent line weights, structured cards, pressure chains, ladders, proof trails, and before/after comparisons.
- **Surface treatment:** warm paper-like background, subtle texture only, soft minimal shadows, meaningful stamps/labels/manual marks only.
- **Allowed variation:** vary slide archetype, framework depth, pressure-chain shape, evidence treatment, headline tone, or single-post accent emphasis. Do not invent a new visual identity.

## Premium analytical identity
The carousel should feel like a practical investor-communication memo. It should translate liquidity pressure into founder behavior without becoming macro commentary or fear-driven market content.

## Visual identity direction
Use a liquidity pressure chain and value-realization ladder. The central visual object is the movement from founder progress to investor belief to eventual value realization.

## Signature accent palette
- Dark Forest Green: credible value path.
- Slate Blue: market evidence and source context.
- Controlled Amber: next-value-step decision gates.
- Muted Red / Rust: stalled liquidity, distribution pressure, or vague progress.
- Deep Green: clearer value realization logic.

## Typography direction
- Display serif: Slide 1 headline and Slide 6 framework title only.
- Clean sans-serif: labels, explanatory notes, and diagram text.
- Mono-style: source tags, caveat labels, and small memo markers only.

## Diagram family
Liquidity pressure chain + value-realization ladder + update rewrite anatomy.

## Plain-language shadows for framework terms
- Liquidity pressure → investors need money to come back, not just paper value to go up.
- Value realization → how progress could eventually become cash, exit, or defensible enterprise value.
- Next buyer belief → what the next investor, acquirer, or public-market buyer must believe.
- Optionality → credible paths forward, not vague future upside.
- Distribution pressure → LPs waiting for capital to return.

## Mobile readability rules
- Avoid dense macro charts.
- Keep the LP → fund → founder chain simple.
- Make the ladder visually save-worthy.
- Keep each slide useful even if read alone.
- Use plain-English labels under every finance term.

## Slide-by-slide visual directions

### Slide 1 — Tension headline
- **Role:** hook.
- **Reader value delivered:** the reader understands that investor behavior is shaped by the investor's own capital-return pressure.
- **Composition archetype:** opposing pressure cards.
- **Dominant element:** founder progress card facing investor liquidity pressure card.
- **Headline mode:** display-serif tension.
- **Signature visual grammar:** rust pressure marker on investor side, green progress marker on founder side, amber gap between them.
- **Minimal text:** “Your investor has a liquidity problem too.”

### Slide 2 — The pressure chain
- **Role:** diagnosis.
- **Reader value delivered:** a simple causal map.
- **Composition archetype:** horizontal chain.
- **Dominant element:** LPs waiting for distributions → fund managers managing returns → founders needing a clearer value path.
- **Headline mode:** plain-language mechanism.
- **Signature visual grammar:** arrows, cards, light rust friction marks at each handoff.
- **Minimal text:** “Investor pressure travels downstream.”

### Slide 3 — Evidence cards
- **Role:** evidence slide direction.
- **Reader value delivered:** current market context with caveats.
- **Composition archetype:** evidence bank.
- **Dominant element:** three Slate Blue cards.
- **Cards:** `Q1 2026 deal and exit value set records`; `Without five largest deals/exits, values fall sharply`; `NVCA: liquidity is still tight`.
- **Headline mode:** factual context.
- **Signature visual grammar:** source tags, caveat footer, no dense macro chart.
- **Caveat:** “Market-level context, not a prediction for one company.”

### Slide 4 — False update vs useful update
- **Role:** antithesis slide direction.
- **Reader value delivered:** a clear communication contrast.
- **Composition archetype:** split-screen update comparison.
- **Dominant element:** left `Progress report` card vs right `Value-path update` card.
- **Headline mode:** contrast headline.
- **Left:** “We grew pipeline.”
- **Right:** “Pipeline grew in the buyer segment most likely to support expansion, partnership, or acquisition logic.”
- **Signature visual grammar:** rust “activity only” label; green “value path” label.

### Slide 5 — Hidden mechanism
- **Role:** hidden mechanism.
- **Reader value delivered:** the reader sees what investors are really listening for.
- **Composition archetype:** belief path.
- **Dominant element:** current investor → next investor → strategic buyer / public market → LP return path.
- **Headline mode:** mechanism reveal.
- **Signature visual grammar:** amber question gate before each future buyer; green path only when belief is specific.
- **Minimal text:** “Investors listen for what the next buyer can believe.”

### Slide 6 — The Value-Realization Ladder
- **Role:** framework slide direction.
- **Reader value delivered:** a reusable narrative tool.
- **Composition archetype:** five-rung ladder.
- **Dominant element:** vertical ladder from activity to value realization.
- **Rungs:** Activity → Signal → Quality → Optionality → Value realization.
- **Plain labels:** What happened? What does it prove? Is it durable? What paths open? Who could value this later?
- **Headline mode:** display-serif framework title.
- **Signature visual grammar:** dark green ladder, amber question tags, deep green top rung.

### Slide 7 — Rewrite pattern
- **Role:** application slide direction.
- **Reader value delivered:** a practical sentence structure for investor updates.
- **Composition archetype:** before/after rewrite anatomy.
- **Dominant element:** update sentence annotated into three parts.
- **Pattern:** `Progress` → `What it proves` → `What value path becomes more credible`.
- **Before:** “Enterprise pipeline increased this month.”
- **After:** “Enterprise pipeline increased in the segment where expansion revenue and strategic partnership interest are most likely, giving us a clearer path to durable value.”
- **Signature visual grammar:** annotate the after sentence with three small labels.
- **Note:** “Fictional example for structure.”

### Slide 8 — Final operating rule
- **Role:** final profile-entry slide direction.
- **Reader value delivered:** a reusable self-audit.
- **Composition archetype:** one-question field-manual card.
- **Dominant element:** large amber question card with quiet green path underneath.
- **Headline mode:** direct operating question.
- **Main text:** “What does this progress make more believable next?”
- **Profile line:** “Operator Leverage OS — visual systems for strategy, investor, and operator clarity.”

## Evidence slide direction
Slide 3 should show why strong headline markets can still contain liquidity pressure. Keep the caveat visible. Do not imply that founders control liquidity cycles.

## Antithesis slide direction
Slide 4 should make the communication behavior obvious: progress alone is weaker than progress connected to a credible value path.

## Framework slide direction
Slide 6 should be the save-worthy tool. It must help founders turn an update from activity reporting into value-realization logic.

## Application slide direction
Slide 7 should give a practical sentence structure for investor updates. Label the example as fictional and structural.

## Final profile-entry slide direction
Slide 8 should create profile intent through a useful self-audit question, not through a paid-product pitch.

## What to avoid
- Doom-and-gloom market tone.
- Overly technical liquidity jargon.
- Generic “exits are hard” commentary.
- Implying founders control liquidity cycles.
- Any new palette, random icons, or finance stock imagery.
- Claims that exits, IPOs, acquisitions, or fundraising outcomes are guaranteed.
- Paid-product bait or income claims.
