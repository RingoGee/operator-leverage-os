# Visual Generation Prompt: Capital Concentration Proof Stack

## Purpose of file
Copy this prompt into an external image/design generation tool to create the first visual attempt for the `Capital Concentration Proof Stack` carousel.

## Usage notes
- Generate visuals only outside Codex.
- Do not add claims, statistics, or source numbers beyond what is listed here.
- Keep the carousel infographic-led and visual-first.

## Full copyable master prompt
Create an 8-slide Instagram carousel for Operator Leverage OS titled “Capital Is Available. Attention Is Not.” The carousel should look like a premium visual strategy memo for founders and founder's office operators, not a generic startup listicle.

Visual style: warm ivory background, charcoal typography and diagram lines, deep navy or forest green for proof accents, one electric blue highlight for investor attention bottlenecks, muted red only for doubt/leakage markers. Use consistent locked slide chrome: top-left `Capital & Narrative`, top-right slide number, bottom-left `Operator Leverage OS`, bottom-right source/caveat marker where relevant. Keep text minimal but high-signal and mobile-readable.

Core story: Current fundraising communication must prove allocation readiness. Venture capital is available, but attention and conviction are concentrated. The visual system should move from a capital funnel to investor allocation gates, evidence cards, a doubt-removal conveyor, and a five-layer Allocation Proof Stack.

Slide sequence:
1. Funnel tension: Large capital pool narrowing into tiny attention gate. Text: “Capital is available. Attention is not.” Subline: “Are you allocatable?”
2. Diagnosis: Three investor gates labeled plausible → diligence-worthy → allocation-ready. Text: “Good is not enough. The question is allocation readiness.”
3. Evidence: Three cards with exact text: “Carta: $119.5B raised in 2025 on Carta”; “Carta: 4,859 rounds — lowest in at least six years”; “NVCA: AI captured 65%+ of 2025 venture investment.” Caveat strip: “Market signal, not a company-level guarantee.”
4. Antithesis: Split screen. Left: “Louder deck: bigger TAM, more logos, more AI language.” Right: “Proof stack: timing, urgency, traction quality, defensibility, capital logic.” Main line: “More impressive ≠ more investable.”
5. Mechanism: Doubt-removal conveyor. Doubt cards enter: why now, urgency, growth quality, moat, use of funds, next proof. Checked proof blocks exit. Text: “Strong slide = one doubt removed.”
6. Framework: Hero slide titled “The Allocation Proof Stack.” Five layers: Market pull — why now? Problem urgency — why must buyers act? Traction quality — why is growth real? Defensibility — why can this compound? Capital logic — why does this round unlock the next proof point?
7. Application: Before/after fundraising narrative card. Before: “We are an AI workflow platform for finance teams.” After: “Finance teams are moving from manual reconciliation to exception-based control. Our wedge proves buyer urgency, usage depth, and the next compliance milestone this round unlocks.” Add tiny note: “Fictional example for structure.”
8. Behavior shift: Large checklist card. Text: “Which investor doubt does this remove?” Add soft profile line: “Operator Leverage OS — visual systems for strategy, investor, and operator clarity.”

## Headline modes
Contrarian tension, diagnosis, evidence label, antithesis, mechanism reveal, named framework, applied rewrite, behavior shift.

## Signature visual grammar
Operating-system diagrams, source cards, sharp labels, clean arrows, muted grids, source caveats, and one recurring accent that tracks the attention bottleneck.

## Global avoid rules
Avoid rocket ships, money bags, generic VC icons, AI as a decorative icon, hype aesthetics, income claims, fake dashboards, or dense paragraph slides. Do not invent data. Do not imply fundraising is easy.

## Final quality guardrails
- Slide 6 must be the most save-worthy slide.
- Slide 3 must include source tags and caveat.
- Every slide must advance the same argument.
- Visuals must explain the logic, not decorate the text.
