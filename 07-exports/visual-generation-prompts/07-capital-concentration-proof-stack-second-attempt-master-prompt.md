## Full copyable master prompt
Create an 8-slide Instagram carousel titled “Capital Is Available. Conviction Is Scarce.”

Audience: founders, founder's office operators, chiefs of staff, and strategy/finance operators preparing fundraising decks, investor updates, or follow-up materials.

Core business point: In the current fundraising market, the problem is not only whether a startup is interesting. The problem is whether an investor can quickly understand the case, believe the proof, and defend the allocation internally. Every slide should help remove one investor doubt.

Practical consequence line to make visible in the deck: “This is why investors keep asking the same follow-up questions — the deck showed activity, but did not remove doubt.”

Use a calm, premium visual strategy memo style. Do not make the carousel look like a startup hype deck, SaaS dashboard, MBA template, motivational poster, or generic AI-tool content.

Slide sequence:

1. Reduced opening / clear tension
   - Visual: A simple split visual, not a complex funnel. Left side: “Capital exists.” Right side: “Conviction is scarce.” Put one small amber gate between them labeled “investor belief.”
   - Text: “Capital is available. Conviction is scarce.”
   - Small support line: “The job of your fundraising story is to make belief easier.”
   - Keep this slide sparse. Do not show the full framework yet.

2. Exact situation / who this is for
   - Visual: A founder deck, investor update, and follow-up memo sitting in one row, all pointing toward an investor decision room.
   - Text: “This is about decks, updates, and follow-ups.”
   - Support line: “If the investor cannot explain the case clearly, the company stays interesting but unfunded.”
   - Make the business situation obvious: fundraising communication, not abstract operator philosophy.

3. Evidence / market context
   - Visual: Three source-backed evidence cards with a calm Slate Blue treatment.
   - Exact card text:
     1. “Carta: $119.5B raised in 2025 on Carta.”
     2. “Carta: 4,859 rounds — lowest annual count in at least six years.”
     3. “NVCA: AI captured 65%+ of 2025 venture investment.”
   - Caveat strip: “Market signal, not a company-level guarantee.”
   - Text: “More capital does not mean more attention for every company.”
   - Do not add extra data.

4. Default behavior vs stronger behavior
   - Visual: Split-screen comparison.
   - Left card: “Default: make the deck more impressive.” Labels: bigger market, more logos, more AI language, more screenshots.
   - Right card: “Better: make the case easier to believe.” Labels: why now, why buyers care, why growth is real, why this can compound, why this round matters.
   - Main line: “Impressive is not the same as believable.”
   - Keep tone respectful, not mocking.

5. Practical consequence / why investors keep asking follow-ups
   - Visual: Investor follow-up loop. Deck goes out → investor asks same questions → founder sends more explanation → conviction still unclear.
   - Text: “This is why investors keep asking the same follow-up questions.”
   - Support line: “The deck showed activity, but did not remove doubt.”
   - Use Muted Red / Rust only for the repeated doubt loop.

6. Save-worthy model / The Proof Stack
   - Visual: Five-layer proof stack with plain-language labels first and abstract terms second, smaller.
   - Title: “The Proof Stack”
   - Layers:
     1. “Why now?” / Market pull
     2. “Why must buyers act?” / Problem urgency
     3. “Is growth real?” / Traction quality
     4. “Can this compound?” / Defensibility
     5. “Why this round?” / Capital logic
   - Subline: “The evidence that removes investor doubt layer by layer.”
   - This should be the most save-worthy slide, but keep it readable.

7. Applied rewrite / from category claim to investor belief
   - Visual: Before/after fundraising narrative card with annotation tags.
   - Before: “We are an AI workflow platform for finance teams.”
   - After: “Finance teams are moving from manual reconciliation to exception-based control. Our wedge proves buyer urgency, usage depth, and the next compliance milestone this round unlocks.”
   - Tags: why now, buyer urgency, growth is real, what this round unlocks.
   - Tiny note: “Fictional example for structure.”
   - Make the rewrite feel practical, not buzzword-heavy.

8. Final behavior shift
   - Visual: A clean one-question audit card over a faint proof-stack outline.
   - Main question: “Which investor doubt does this remove?”
   - Checklist:
     - Why now?
     - Why must buyers act?
     - Is growth real?
     - Can this compound?
     - Why this round?
   - Soft profile line: “Operator Leverage OS — visual systems for strategy, investor, and operator clarity.”
   - No hard product CTA.
