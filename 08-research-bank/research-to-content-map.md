# Research to Content Map

## Purpose

This file explains how research inputs become Operator Leverage OS content without overstating evidence or turning weak examples into broad claims.

The goal is to help future Codex research scans move from sources, claims, examples, and patterns into topic-bank candidates, carousel briefs, evidence slides, and product insight signals.

## Mapping levels

1. **Source** — the original report, article, paper, dataset, public event, company example, or credible reference.
2. **Claim** — a specific statement, finding, statistic, or conclusion extracted from a source.
3. **Example** — a public company/event example, workplace pattern, mini-case, or observed situation.
4. **Pattern** — a repeated theme across sources, examples, audience signals, or field observations.
5. **Content angle** — the operator-facing interpretation that could become a post, carousel, or note.
6. **Topic-bank candidate** — a structured content idea ready to be added to `04-content-bank/topic-bank.csv` if approved.
7. **Carousel brief** — the doctrine-led argument plan for a carousel.
8. **Evidence slide** — a slide that uses the research honestly as a data point, public example, observed pattern, synthesis, or illustrative example.
9. **Product insight** — a signal that may inform a future template, system, SOP, audit, or implementation asset.

## Evidence label rules

Use accurate reader-facing labels.

| Evidence type | Reader-facing label |
| --- | --- |
| Research-backed statistic | “Research-backed signal” or “Data point” |
| Public company/event example | “Public example” |
| Workplace pattern | “Observed pattern” |
| Illustrative constructed example | “Illustrative workplace example” |
| Synthesis from multiple sources | “Synthesis” |
| Unsourced hypothesis | “Working hypothesis” |

Use “not a sourced statistic” as an internal note only, not as prominent slide copy.

## Content mapping table

| Research input | Use in content | Visible label | Caveat required? | Example use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Credible statistic from a named source | Evidence slide, hook support, or trend context | Research-backed signal / Data point | Yes | “A data point showing why this operator problem matters.” |
| Public company or event example | Mini-case or applied example | Public example | Yes | “A visible example of the mechanism in the real world.” |
| Repeated workplace pattern | Diagnosis, antithesis, or mechanism slide | Observed pattern | Yes | “A pattern operators recognize from work.” |
| Constructed workplace scenario | Mini-case or application slide | Illustrative workplace example | Yes | “A concrete example that explains the idea without pretending to be research.” |
| Multi-source synthesis | First-principles diagnosis or trend framing | Synthesis | Yes | “A recurring theme across several sources.” |
| Unsourced hypothesis | Research prompt or future validation note | Working hypothesis | Yes | “A claim to test before turning into content.” |

## When research is strong enough for a slide

Research is strong enough for visible slide use when:

- The source is credible.
- The source is recent enough for the claim.
- The claim directly supports the argument.
- The caveat is understood.
- No exaggeration is required.
- The evidence label is accurate.

## When research should not be used

Do not use research in a slide when it has:

- Weak source quality.
- An outdated source for a current trend.
- Unclear methodology.
- An anecdote presented as data.
- A statistic without context.
- A source that does not support the claim.

## How research feeds skills

- **OLS Research Scan** populates the research bank.
- **OLS Idea Generator** uses the research bank for topic candidates.
- **OLS Carousel Brief Builder** uses claims/examples for evidence layers.
- **OLS Product Bridge Reviewer** can use repeated research patterns and audience demand signals.
