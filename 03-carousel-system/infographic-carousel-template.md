## Default 8-slide argument arc
This is the default arc for important thought-leadership carousels, not a rigid rule. Some carousels may need 6, 7, 9, or 10 slides, but important thought pieces should usually have enough space to develop the argument.

### Slide 1: Tension / question
- **Argument role:** Create an unresolved question, status threat, contrarian claim, diagnostic tension, surprising contrast, or named problem.
- **Visual move:** Tension visual.
- **Structured text:** One sharp tension, not a summary of the answer.

### Slide 2: First-principles diagnosis
- **Argument role:** Explain why the problem exists structurally.
- **Visual move:** Root-cause map, issue tree, ambiguity zone, or leakage map.
- **Structured text:** Short explanation of the cause.

### Slide 3: Evidence / data / example
- **Argument role:** Ground the argument in proof, research, example, benchmark, mini-case, or observable pattern.
- **Visual move:** Evidence card, mini-case card, or benchmark callout.
- **Structured text:** Claim supported, source/example, caveat if needed.

### Slide 4: Antithesis / contrast
- **Argument role:** Show default behavior versus high-leverage behavior.
- **Visual move:** Antithesis table, before/after system, or split-screen comparison.
- **Structured text:** Contrast labels that clarify the behavior gap.

### Slide 5: Hidden mechanism
- **Argument role:** Reveal the unseen process that creates the outcome difference.
- **Visual move:** Mechanism diagram, feedback loop, waterfall, or value-leakage diagram.
- **Structured text:** Explain where value, trust, clarity, proof, or decision quality leaks.

### Slide 6: Framework or decision tool
- **Argument role:** Give the reader a reusable model.
- **Visual move:** Framework model, decision tree, scorecard, matrix, or control panel.
- **Structured text:** Labels and short guidance that explain how to use it.

### Slide 7: Application / worked example
- **Argument role:** Show how the model changes a real work situation.
- **Visual move:** Worked example, before/after system, proof trail, or operator note.
- **Structured text:** Concrete scenario with practical interpretation.

### Slide 8: Behavior shift + profile reason
- **Argument role:** Leave the reader with a changed operating question and a reason to follow or visit the profile.
- **Visual move:** Behavior checklist, operating question, profile-entry slide, or “so what?” callout.
- **Structured text:** One behavior shift plus category promise.

## Final check before design
- [ ] The carousel starts with tension, not a tool.
- [ ] The exact operator problem is clear.
- [ ] The first-principles cause is clear.
- [ ] Evidence, data, or a concrete example supports the argument where relevant.
- [ ] The common false solution is named.
- [ ] The antithesis is respectful and useful.
- [ ] The hidden mechanism explains why the problem repeats.
- [ ] The framework explains a mechanism, not just common sense.
- [ ] The visual moves develop one central argument.
- [ ] Slide text is structured and high-signal.
- [ ] Each slide creates a reason to swipe.
- [ ] The final slide includes a behavior shift and profile-entry reason.
- [ ] AI or other tools are secondary implementation layers.
- [ ] Any paid bridge is optional and implementation-focused.
