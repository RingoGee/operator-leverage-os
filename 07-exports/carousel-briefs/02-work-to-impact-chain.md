# Carousel Brief: Work-to-Impact Chain

## Working title
Work-to-Impact Chain

## Pillar
Corporate Leverage

## Audience segment
Corporate professionals, strategy and growth operators, consultants, and founder's office professionals who do useful work but struggle to make the value visible.

## Real operator problem
People complete meaningful work, but when it is time to explain impact, they only have scattered tasks, updates, and outputs.

## Hidden thinking flaw
They assume completed work automatically communicates value. In reality, work only creates leverage when it is connected to a visible chain of problem, action, outcome, proof, and next decision.

## Mental model
The Work-to-Impact Chain: Work → Output → Outcome → Proof → Leverage.

## Central visual object
Work-to-impact chain.

## Why this topic belongs early in the launch sequence
This introduces one of the brand's most important ideas: useful work is not enough unless it becomes legible proof. It also creates a natural soft bridge to Proof Stack without turning the post into a product pitch.

## Recommended 7-slide visual sequence

| Slide | Visual purpose | Visual object or layout | Minimal slide text direction | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Show scattered work | Cluster of tasks with no connecting line | Short problem label | Make the pain recognizable. |
| 2 | Reveal the hidden flaw | Broken chain between work and impact | One sentence naming the flaw | Do not blame the audience; show the missing system. |
| 3 | Introduce the mental model | Linear chain with five stages | Name the chain | Keep each stage visually distinct. |
| 4 | Show the full chain | Work → Output → Outcome → Proof → Leverage | Labels only | This is the central visual object. |
| 5 | Apply to one example | One task moved through the chain | Short before/after labels | Use a reporting cleanup or stakeholder update example. |
| 6 | Show the behavior shift | From activity tracking to proof capture | One practical takeaway | Connect work to decision-maker value. |
| 7 | Save/apply/bridge | Chain plus optional Proof Stack bridge | Save/apply/paid bridge pattern | Keep bridge soft and implementation-focused. |

## Minimal slide text direction
Keep text to short labels for each chain stage. Avoid long explanations of impact; let the chain do the teaching.

## Small applied example
A reporting cleanup becomes more valuable when framed as: messy reporting problem → cleaned report output → faster stakeholder decisions → fewer review errors → stronger proof of operational leverage.

## Optional paid asset bridge to Proof Stack
- **Paid asset bridge:** Proof Stack.
- **Why it fits:** The free post teaches the chain; Proof Stack would help capture the Evidence Bank, Work-to-Impact Chain, and Weekly Signal Panel consistently.
- **Boundary:** Do not imply the paid asset is required to understand the model.

## CTA pattern, not final CTA copy
Save for later / Diagnose your work / Paid asset bridge.

## Visual notes
- Use one horizontal chain or vertical flow.
- Each chain stage should have one icon-like label, but avoid decorative icons that do not explain the idea.
- Visibility is the benefit; proof is the system.

## Quality checklist
- [ ] Starts with an operator problem, not a tool.
- [ ] Hidden thinking flaw is clear.
- [ ] Mental model is reusable.
- [ ] One central visual object carries the explanation.
- [ ] Slide text is minimal.
- [ ] Tools remain secondary or absent.
- [ ] Paid bridge to Proof Stack is optional and implementation-focused.
