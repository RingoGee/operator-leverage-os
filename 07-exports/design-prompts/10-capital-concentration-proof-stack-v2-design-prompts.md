# Design Prompts: Capital Concentration Proof Stack v2

## Purpose
Create a design-ready visual prompt for a Capital & Narrative carousel. This file is for visual direction only. Do not generate images, designed slides, Canva files, or visual execution packs from this step.

## Working title
Capital Is Available. Conviction Is Scarce.

## One-sentence point
Founders should design every deck, update, and follow-up to remove a specific investor doubt, because current venture capital is concentrating around companies investors can understand, believe, and defend internally.

## Current source context
Use this context carefully and with caveats:

- Carta reported that startups on its platform raised `$119.5B` in 2025, while the annual round count fell to `4,859` rounds.
- NVCA's 2026 Yearbook says the 2025 U.S. venture market deployed `$320B`, with AI accounting for `65.4%` of deal value.
- NVCA's Q1 2026 PitchBook-NVCA Venture Monitor summary says headline deal and exit values were highly concentrated in the five largest deals/exits.

Sources:

- Carta, State of Private Markets: 2025 in Review: https://carta.com/data/state-of-private-markets-q4-2025/
- NVCA, 2026 Yearbook: https://nvca.org/2026-nvca-yearbook/
- NVCA, Q1 2026 PitchBook-NVCA Venture Monitor: https://nvca.org/pitchbook-nvca-venture-monitor/

## Brand Identity Lock v1
Use the locked Operator Leverage OS identity:

- **Palette:** Warm Ivory / Soft Paper White background; Deep Charcoal / Near Black text and linework; Dark Forest Green main accent; Slate Blue evidence cards; Proof Orange / Controlled Amber for gates and key questions; Muted Red / Rust for unresolved doubt; Deep Green for proof accepted.
- **Typography:** display serif for major conceptual headlines; clean modern sans-serif for body, labels, diagrams, notes, and source lines; optional mono-style only for source tags, memo codes, or field-manual markers.
- **Chrome:** stable top-left `Capital & Narrative`, top-right slide number, optional memo/category label, bottom-left `Operator Leverage OS`, optional footer source/caveat line on evidence slide only, consistent safe margins and baseline alignment.
- **Spacing:** generous margins, clean separation between headline, explanation, and visual object, mobile-first sizing, no unreadable tiny labels.
- **Diagram language:** clean line diagrams, restrained icons, consistent line weights, structured cards, decision gates, proof trails, and before/after comparisons.
- **Surface treatment:** warm paper-like background, subtle texture only, soft minimal shadows, meaningful stamps/labels/manual marks only.
- **Allowed variation:** vary slide archetype, visual metaphor, framework depth, evidence treatment, diagram type, headline tone, or single-post accent emphasis. Do not invent a new visual identity.

## Premium analytical identity
The carousel should feel like an investor communication field manual: calm, sharp, boardroom-ready, and practical. The design must make the fundraising behavior clearer, not merely make the slides look premium.

## Locked slide chrome system
- Top-left: `Capital & Narrative`.
- Top-right: `01 / 08` through `08 / 08`.
- Bottom-left: `Operator Leverage OS`.
- Bottom-right: source/caveat marker only on Slide 3.
- Keep chrome quiet and fixed. Never let diagrams invade the header or footer zones.

## Visual identity direction
Use a proof-trail visual system: doubt enters as an amber or rust question, moves through proof cards, and exits as deep-green conviction. The central visual object is a belief gate supported by a layered proof stack.

## Signature accent palette
- Dark Forest Green: the proof path.
- Slate Blue: source-backed evidence cards.
- Controlled Amber: the belief gate and audit questions.
- Muted Red / Rust: unresolved investor doubts and repeated follow-up loops.
- Deep Green: proof accepted or conviction strengthened.

## Typography direction
- Display serif: Slide 1 headline and Slide 6 framework title only.
- Clean sans-serif: labels, questions, source notes, and support text.
- Mono-style: source tags and tiny caveat labels only.

## Diagram family
Belief gate + proof trail + before/after rewrite anatomy.

## Plain-language shadows for framework terms
- Allocation readiness → ready to earn investor conviction.
- Traction quality → proof that growth is real, not noisy.
- Capital logic → why this amount, and what it unlocks.
- Next proof → what we will prove next.
- Proof stack → evidence that removes investor doubt layer by layer.
- Investor doubt → the unanswered question stopping conviction.

## Mobile readability rules
- One dominant object per slide.
- Keep evidence cards to 1 number + 1 plain-English implication.
- Avoid dense market charts.
- Use 1-second headline, 3-second structure, 8-second detail hierarchy.
- Make Slide 6 save-worthy without making the labels tiny.

## Slide-by-slide visual directions

### Slide 1 — Reduced tension
- **Role:** hook.
- **Reader value delivered:** the reader immediately sees that the problem is not only capital supply; it is investor conviction.
- **Composition archetype:** two-panel tension.
- **Dominant element:** `Capital exists` card → amber belief gate → `Conviction is scarce` card.
- **Headline mode:** display-serif thesis.
- **Signature visual grammar:** controlled amber gate, dark green path, large whitespace.
- **Minimal slide text:** “Capital is available. Conviction is scarce.” / “Make belief easier, not the deck louder.”

### Slide 2 — Exact situation
- **Role:** domain grounding.
- **Reader value delivered:** the reader knows this is about decks, investor updates, and follow-up materials.
- **Composition archetype:** artifact flow.
- **Dominant element:** three artifact cards: deck, update, follow-up memo → investor decision room.
- **Headline mode:** plain sans-serif operating statement.
- **Signature visual grammar:** cards, arrow path, quiet field-manual labels.
- **Minimal slide text:** “This is about decks, updates, and follow-ups.”

### Slide 3 — Evidence context
- **Role:** evidence slide direction.
- **Reader value delivered:** the reader sees why concentration raises the communication bar.
- **Composition archetype:** three evidence cards with caveat footer.
- **Dominant element:** Slate Blue evidence bank.
- **Headline mode:** factual context.
- **Evidence cards:** `Carta: $119.5B raised in 2025`; `Carta: 4,859 rounds`; `NVCA: AI captured 65.4% of 2025 VC deal value`.
- **Signature visual grammar:** source tags, small caveat line, no decorative chart.
- **Caveat:** “Market signal, not a company-level guarantee.”

### Slide 4 — Default vs better behavior
- **Role:** antithesis slide direction.
- **Reader value delivered:** a clear behavior rewrite.
- **Composition archetype:** split-screen before/after.
- **Dominant element:** left rust pile of “more proof” assets vs right green stack that answers one doubt at a time.
- **Headline mode:** contrast headline.
- **Signature visual grammar:** rust false path, deep-green improved path, small “operator rewrite” stamp.
- **Minimal slide text:** “Default: add more material. Better: remove more doubt.”

### Slide 5 — Practical consequence
- **Role:** hidden mechanism.
- **Reader value delivered:** the reader sees why vague traction creates follow-up loops.
- **Composition archetype:** loop diagram.
- **Dominant element:** unanswered question loop: interesting company → unclear proof → partner follow-up → stalled conviction.
- **Headline mode:** mechanism reveal.
- **Signature visual grammar:** rust loop breaking into green proof trail.
- **Minimal slide text:** “Unanswered doubts create follow-up loops.”

### Slide 6 — The Conviction Proof Stack
- **Role:** framework slide direction.
- **Reader value delivered:** a reusable audit tool for investor materials.
- **Composition archetype:** layered stack / vertical checklist.
- **Dominant element:** five-layer proof stack.
- **Layers:** Market pull → Traction quality → Business model logic → Capital use → Next proof.
- **Headline mode:** display-serif framework title.
- **Signature visual grammar:** green stack layers, amber audit question at each layer, optional checkmark only after proof is specific.
- **Minimal slide text:** “Do not show progress. Show which doubt the progress removes.”

### Slide 7 — Rewrite pattern
- **Role:** application slide direction.
- **Reader value delivered:** a practical sentence-level improvement.
- **Composition archetype:** before/after rewrite anatomy.
- **Dominant element:** three-part sentence structure.
- **Pattern:** `Signal` → `Why it is credible` → `Which investor doubt it removes`.
- **Headline mode:** practical instruction.
- **Signature visual grammar:** annotate the “after” sentence with three small labels.
- **Example note:** label as “Fictional example for structure.”

### Slide 8 — Final operating question
- **Role:** final profile-entry slide direction.
- **Reader value delivered:** a reusable self-audit before sending investor material.
- **Composition archetype:** field-manual card.
- **Dominant element:** one large amber question card on warm paper.
- **Headline mode:** direct question.
- **Signature visual grammar:** quiet Operator Leverage OS footer, no hard sell.
- **Main text:** “Which investor doubt does this remove?”
- **Profile line:** “Operator Leverage OS — visual systems for strategy, investor, and operator clarity.”

## Evidence slide direction
Slide 3 must be source-backed, caveated, and visually simple. Use the numbers only to establish market context. Do not imply that a specific company will raise capital because these market-level numbers exist.

## Antithesis slide direction
Slide 4 should respectfully contrast the common founder move with the better operator move. Avoid mocking founders. The point is behavior change: less volume, more doubt removal.

## Framework slide direction
Slide 6 should be the most save-worthy slide. The stack must be usable as a pre-send audit for a deck, update, or follow-up memo.

## Application slide direction
Slide 7 should turn the framework into a concrete rewrite pattern. Keep it short enough that a founder can copy the structure into a real investor update.

## Final profile-entry slide direction
Slide 8 should create profile intent by making the reader want more investor-communication systems. Do not push a paid asset in the free carousel.

## What to avoid
- Generic fundraising tips.
- Abstract “capital is concentrated” commentary without a communication action.
- Dense market charts.
- Random AI-tool or SaaS icons.
- New palette, decorative gradients, or stock finance imagery.
- Claims that fundraising success is guaranteed.
- Paid-product bait or income claims.
