# Visual Generation Prompt: Liquidity Pressure Narrative

## Purpose of file
Copy this prompt into an external image/design generation tool to create the first visual attempt for the `Liquidity Pressure Narrative` carousel.

## Usage notes
- Generate visuals only outside Codex.
- Do not generate images or designed slides in this repository.
- Do not add claims about guaranteed exits, IPOs, markups, or acquisitions.

## Full copyable master prompt
Create an 8-slide Instagram carousel for Operator Leverage OS titled “Investors Are Not Just Buying Growth. They Are Managing Liquidity Pressure.” The carousel should feel like a calm investment-memo diagram for founders and founder's office operators.

Visual style: warm memo-paper background, charcoal typography and diagram lines, deep green or navy accents for credible value path, muted red for liquidity pressure. Avoid panic aesthetics. Use consistent locked chrome: top-left `Capital & Narrative`, top-right slide number, bottom-left `Operator Leverage OS`, bottom-right source/caveat marker on evidence slide.

Core story: In a liquidity-constrained venture environment, investor communication gets stronger when growth is connected to quality, efficiency, optionality, and credible value realization. The visual system should move from a pressure chart to LP/fund/founder cascade, market evidence, next-buyer belief chain, and the five-rung Liquidity-Aware Narrative Ladder.

Slide sequence:
1. Tension chart: A rising growth line compressed by a block labeled “liquidity pressure.” Text: “Investors are not just buying growth.” Support: “They are managing liquidity pressure.”
2. Cascade map: Three levels: “LPs want distributions” → “Funds need credible return paths” → “Founders must make value legible.” Use downward pressure arrows and upward proof arrows. Text: “Your investor has investors too.”
3. Evidence stack: Three cards. Card 1: “NVCA: 2025 venture-backed exits more than doubled YoY, but remained insufficient for backlog.” Card 2: “NVCA: 859 unicorns valued at $4.34T; 30–40 unicorn exits last year.” Card 3: “PitchBook-NVCA: Q1 2026 records were highly concentrated in the five largest deals/exits.” Caveat: “Market context, not a company-level prediction.”
4. Antithesis: Split screen. Left: “Upside story: bigger TAM, bigger round, bigger future.” Right: “Value-realization path: milestones, quality, efficiency, optionality, next buyer logic.” Main line: “Bigger ambition ≠ clearer value path.”
5. Mechanism chain: Current investor → next investor → strategic buyer → LP. Each card asks: “What must they believe?” Text: “Investors listen for the next buyer's belief.”
6. Framework: Hero ladder titled “The Liquidity-Aware Narrative Ladder.” Five rungs: Growth — is there real demand? Quality — is it durable? Efficiency — does capital convert into proof? Optionality — are there credible paths forward? Realization — who could value this, and why?
7. Application: Before/after investor update sentence. Before: “We grew enterprise pipeline.” After: “Enterprise pipeline grew in the segment most likely to support expansion revenue, strategic partnerships, and future acquisition logic.” Add tags: quality, optionality, realization. Tiny note: “Fictional example for structure.”
8. Behavior shift: Large prompt card over a faint ladder watermark. Text: “What does this progress make more believable next?” Profile line: “Operator Leverage OS — visual systems for strategy, fundraising, and executive communication.”

## Headline modes
Contrarian claim, structural diagnosis, evidence label, antithesis, mechanism reveal, named model, applied example, behavior shift.

## Signature visual grammar
Memo-paper grids, pressure arrows, source cards, next-buyer chain, ladder framework, minimal icons, calm finance-style diagrams.

## Global avoid rules
Avoid panic visuals, IPO hype, generic finance stock imagery, guarantees about exits or acquisitions, rocket ships, cash icons, dense finance jargon, and text-heavy slides.

## Final quality guardrails
- Slide 6 must be the most save-worthy framework slide.
- Slide 3 must include source labels and caveat.
- Growth must be shown as necessary but incomplete, not irrelevant.
- The carousel must teach communication discipline, not investment advice.
